## Not sure where to start? Here is a real run's output

![Gut community composition across six samples: Bacteroides high in adults, Bifidobacterium high in children](./imgs/taxprofiler-composition.svg)

*Figure 1 — Read share by major group across the six samples. Percentages are of total reads, including the unclassified fraction.*

The three child samples carry **Bifidobacterium** at 26.5–42.7% of reads; the three adult samples have almost none (0–1.9%). **Bacteroides** runs the other way: 7.1–42.3% in adults, 0–12.4% in children. At species level, the child samples are led by *Bifidobacterium longum*, *B. bifidum* and *B. catenulatum*; the adult samples by *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*, *B. fragilis* and *Phocaeicola vulgatus*.

The direction of this difference matches the original study, where Bacteroidetes were higher in adults and young children carried more Actinobacteria. It is a check that the workflow runs correctly, not a new finding: three samples per group, group labels taken from the submitting group's sample titles (ENA carries no age field), and a single database.

## Run at scale: 1,200 samples or more, and stay reproducible

The run in this post is **one batch of six samples**. For cohorts of thousands, the platform provides **LIMS/Cohort**: samples are grouped into cohorts, the samplesheet is generated from the sample list, and the pipeline is triggered automatically from a saved configuration — same parameters, only the raw data changes per sample. Data can also be synced from the sequencer.

As an example: **1,200 samples become 200 batches of six**, each batch using the same manifest. The 200 is arithmetic from this run, not a commitment on time — larger batches, deeper data or a 200 GB database need more resources.

The pipeline stays `nf-core/taxprofiler`, with no source changes. Each batch's manifest records the version, parameters and resources — enough for a reviewer to replay exactly that configuration.

## Comprehensive analysis and expansion

Taxonomic profiling answers "which taxa are in this sample". It is the step that makes everything after it possible: group comparisons, resistance genes, functional analysis, strain-level work. Three things to know before using this result for a real project:

- **The database is at the minimum.** The 8 GB set is the smallest of Kraken2's standard builds, and the one used here was built in 2021. A full set can reach 200 GB, and gut-specific references (UHGG, GTDB) need a current version. This is why 34–63% of reads in the adult samples stayed unclassified.
- **The data here is shallow.** Each sample is only 1.7–1.9 GB, roughly 12 million read pairs. A real cohort is usually deeper, larger, and comes with metadata.
- **Resource needs depend on the input.** Three things decide them: sample count and sequencing depth, database size, and the machine configuration. Six shallow samples are not representative of a cohort.

A full cohort adds: a database chosen for the sample type, batch runs, rarefaction and diversity statistics, resistance gene and functional modules, and the delivery work for a manuscript.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to install anything?**
No — and you do not need a powerful machine either. The pipeline, containers and reference database run in the workspace; your machine only needs a browser.

**Can I use a larger database?**
Yes. The 8 GB set is the minimum for a trial; a 200 GB set or a gut-specific reference works on the same workflow, with more storage and a longer first load.

**How is a large cohort run?**
In batches, through LIMS/Cohort: 1,200 samples are 200 batches of six, sharing one configuration and one manifest. Resource needs depend on sample count, depth and database size, so they should be estimated on the specific data.

**Can I run my own data?**
Yes, for data that does not contain sensitive personal data. For human or clinical data, check the service scope first; the appropriate arrangement is usually a self-hosted deployment on your own infrastructure — see [Custom solutions](/en/solutions/custom-solutions).

**One MultiQC bug.** The Kraken/Bracken panel reports `% Top 1 = 0.04%` for `ERR9904444`, while that sample's Bracken table puts *B. thetaiotaomicron* at 35.3%.
